Improving Page Views: Get Visitors to Read More of Your Site

Page views grow when visitors stay and explore. Learn how internal linking, related content, and clear next steps turn one visit into several page views.

Why page views matter

Page views measure how much of your site a visitor explores. A visitor who reads one article and leaves has seen one page; a visitor who reads three has seen three, and is three times as likely to remember you, subscribe, or buy. Page views are not the same as traffic: you can grow page views without growing visitors, simply by helping the people who already arrive to find more of what they want. For sites that earn from advertising, more page views per visit directly means more revenue. For every site, they mean a deeper relationship with the reader.

Make the next page obvious

The simplest way to increase page views is to show the visitor what to read next. Add a related articles section at the end of every post, with three genuinely relevant links and honest titles. Link within the text to your best articles where they naturally fit, using descriptive link text. Feature your most useful pages on the homepage and in the navigation, so a new visitor can find them without searching. The goal is to make the next click the easiest action on the page, not to hide your content behind menus and dead ends.

Write articles that lead onwards

An article that answers a question completely and then stops gives the reader no reason to continue. An article that answers the question and then points to the natural next question keeps them reading. If you explain how to check traffic, link to your guide on growing traffic. If you explain Analytics setup, link to your guide on reading Search Console. This is not manipulation; it is good service, because the reader's next question is predictable and you have already written the answer. Series and topic hubs work the same way at a larger scale.

Speed and readability

A slow page loses readers before they reach the related links at the bottom. A page that is hard to read on mobile loses them sooner. Keep pages fast, use clear headings and short paragraphs, and make links easy to tap. Remove popups that block the content and force the reader to dismiss them before they can continue. Every friction on the page costs page views, because the reader who is annoyed leaves instead of exploring. Test your pages on a real phone, not just on a desktop, because that is how most readers arrive.

Measure and improve

In Analytics, look at views per session and at the pages report to see which pages attract further reading and which are dead ends. A page with high views but low onward clicks needs better related links. A page with high entrances and immediate exits needs a better introduction or a faster load. Improve the worst pages first, because a small fix on a popular page affects more readers than a perfect fix on a page nobody visits. Review quarterly, and keep a note of which changes worked, so the next improvement starts from evidence rather than guesswork.

Frequently asked questions

How many page views per session is good?

It depends on the site type. Content sites often see 1.5 to 2.5 views per session. Compare your own trend over time rather than chasing a universal number.

Do page views affect SEO?

Not directly, but the behaviour behind them — longer visits and more exploration — reflects content that satisfies readers, which search engines value.

Should I split articles into many pages?

No. Splitting an article to inflate page views frustrates readers and is penalised by search engines. Earn extra views with genuinely related content.
