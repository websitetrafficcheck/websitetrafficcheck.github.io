Google Search Console Traffic Guide: Read Clicks, Impressions, and Queries

Search Console shows how Google sees your site. Learn to read the performance report, find opportunities, and fix the pages that appear but are never clicked.

What Search Console measures

Google Search Console is a free tool that shows how your site appears in Google search. Its performance report shows clicks, impressions, average click-through rate, and average position for your pages and for the queries that trigger them. A click is a visit from search. An impression is an appearance in results, whether or not it was clicked. Position is where your page ranked on average. Together these four numbers describe your search visibility completely, and they are measured by Google itself, so they are the most reliable search data you will ever have for your own site.

Reading the performance report

Start by setting the date range to the last three months and comparing it with the previous period, so you can see whether clicks and impressions are rising or falling. Then look at the pages tab to see which pages earn the most clicks, and the queries tab to see which searches bring people in. A page with many impressions and few clicks is appearing in results but not being chosen, which usually means its title or description needs work. A page with a good click-through rate but a low position is one click away from much more traffic, because moving from position 8 to position 3 can triple its clicks.

Finding opportunities

The best opportunities are queries where you rank in positions 5 to 15 with decent impressions. These pages are close to the first page or the top of it, and a modest improvement — a better title, a clearer answer, a new section, or a few internal links — can move them up. Look also for queries where you appear for a question you have not deliberately answered: those reveal demand you did not know existed, and a new section or article can capture it. Sort by impressions, not just clicks, because impressions show demand even when your page is not yet being clicked.

Fixing pages that never get clicked

A page with high impressions and a low click-through rate is a page that searchers see and skip. The commonest cause is a title that does not match the query, or a description that is vague or truncated. Rewrite the title to include the query's main terms and to promise the specific answer the page gives. Rewrite the description to say plainly what the visitor will find. Check that the page's content delivers on that promise in the first screen, because a click that bounces immediately does not help you. Small changes here often produce the fastest traffic gains on a site.

A monthly routine

Check Search Console monthly, not daily, because daily numbers are noisy and rankings move slowly. Each month, note the total clicks and impressions, list the five queries that gained the most, list the five pages with high impressions and low click-through rate, and pick two pages to improve. Make the improvement, record the date, and check the result next month. Over a year, this routine compounds into a large gain, because each improvement brings more data, which reveals the next opportunity. Search Console is not just a reporting tool; it is a work list.

Frequently asked questions

How is Search Console different from Analytics?

Search Console shows how people find you in Google search, including queries and positions. Analytics shows what they do after they arrive. Use both together.

Why do I have impressions but no clicks?

Your page appears in results but searchers choose another result, often because its title or description is less appealing, or because it ranks too low to be noticed.

How long does Search Console data take to appear?

Usually one to two days. Recent data is provisional and can change slightly as Google finalises it.
