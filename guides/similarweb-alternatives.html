SimilarWeb Alternatives: 7 Ways to Estimate Competitor Traffic

SimilarWeb is not the only way to estimate a competitor's traffic. Compare seven alternatives — free and paid — and learn when each one is useful.

Why look beyond SimilarWeb

SimilarWeb is the best-known traffic estimation tool, and its free tier is a reasonable starting point. But it is not the only option, and it is not always the best one for a particular question. Its estimates are weakest for small sites, its free tier hides detail, and its numbers are a model output, not a measurement. Different tools use different data and different models, so comparing two or three of them gives a better sense of the range than trusting one alone. The alternatives below cover search-based estimates, backlink-based estimates, and direct signals, and most have a free tier worth trying.

Semrush and Ahrefs

Semrush and Ahrefs are primarily search and backlink tools, but both publish traffic estimates derived from keyword rankings. They estimate how much search traffic a site receives by looking at the keywords it ranks for, the search volume of those keywords, and its position for each one. This method is strong for sites that depend on search, and weak for sites whose traffic comes mainly from social media or direct visits. Both tools are paid, with limited free trials, and both are worth the cost if search is central to your work, because their keyword data is useful far beyond traffic estimation.

Google Trends and Search Console data

Google Trends does not show traffic, but it shows relative search interest over time for a topic or a brand name, which is a useful proxy when you are comparing two competitors. If a brand's search interest is rising while a rival's is flat, that usually reflects real audience growth. For your own site, Search Console is far better than any estimator: it shows actual clicks and impressions from Google. For a competitor, you can use public search data, such as which keywords they rank for in a free rank checker, to infer roughly how visible they are.

BuiltWith, social signals, and direct observation

BuiltWith and similar tools show what technology a site uses, which sometimes hints at its scale, such as whether it uses an enterprise analytics or advertising platform. Social signals are cruder but visible to everyone: follower counts, engagement on posts, comments, and shares. A site with an active, engaged audience usually has real traffic, while a site with inflated follower counts and no engagement probably does not. Direct observation matters too: how often does the site publish, how fast does it load, how many ads does it run. A site that publishes daily and sells advertising is usually earning enough traffic to justify the effort.

Free checkers and our estimator

Free traffic checkers, including the estimator on this site, give an illustrative range rather than a precise number. That honesty is a feature. A free tool that presents a single exact number is dressing up a guess. Our estimator explains its method, shows a range, and tells you how to check traffic properly with Analytics and Search Console. Use it for a quick sense of scale, then confirm with a paid tool if the decision matters. For most small comparisons, a range and a trend are all you need, and a free tool provides both.

Frequently asked questions

Which SimilarWeb alternative is best for small sites?

None is very accurate for small sites. Use two or three tools together and treat the range as the answer, not any single number.

Are free traffic estimators reliable?

They are reliable as rough guides to scale. They are not reliable as exact measurements, and any tool that claims exact numbers is overstating its accuracy.

Do I need a paid tool?

Only if traffic estimates drive a spending decision, such as buying a site or an ad placement. For learning and planning, free tools and Search Console are usually enough.
