Google Analytics 4 Setup Guide for Beginners

Set up Google Analytics 4 on your website step by step: create the property, install the tag, check that data is arriving, and learn the first reports to read.

Create your account and property

Start at analytics.google.com and sign in with a Google account. Create an account for your business or yourself, then create a property for the website. Give the property the site's name, choose its timezone and currency, and answer the short setup questions about your business size and goals. Google will then offer you a data stream for the web. Enter your site's URL, give the stream a name, and Analytics will generate a measurement ID that looks like G-XXXXXXXXXX and a tag to install on your site. Keep that ID safe; you will need it in the next step.

Install the tag on your site

The tag is a short snippet of JavaScript that sends pageview and event data to Analytics. If your site runs on WordPress, the simplest method is the Site Kit plugin, which connects Analytics without you touching code. On other platforms, look for an Analytics or Google tag setting and paste the measurement ID there. If you edit code directly, paste the tag immediately after the opening head tag on every page, or use Google Tag Manager to manage it centrally. Do not paste the tag in two places at once, because that double-counts your traffic.

Check that data is arriving

After installation, open your site in a browser and visit a few pages. Then go to Analytics, open Reports, then Realtime, and look for your visit. If you see yourself in the Realtime report, the tag is working. If you do not, wait a few minutes and check again, then verify that the measurement ID is correct, that the tag is on every page, and that no consent banner or ad blocker is stopping it. Note that data in standard reports takes up to a day to appear, so Realtime is the right place to check on day one.

Set up the basics that matter

Once data is flowing, turn on the settings that make the data useful. Enable enhanced measurement, which tracks scrolls, outbound clicks, file downloads, and video engagement automatically. Set up conversions for the actions that matter to you, such as a purchase, a form submission, or a newsletter signup. If your site has internal search, enable site search reporting. And create a simple exploration or a Looker Studio dashboard for the metrics you check weekly, so you do not have to dig through reports each time.

The first reports to read

Start with the traffic acquisition report, which shows where your visitors come from: search, social, direct, or referral. Then look at the pages and screens report to see which content people actually read. Then check engagement: average engagement time per session and the engaged sessions rate tell you whether visitors find your content useful. Check these weekly, not daily, because daily numbers are noisy. Over a month, patterns emerge: which sources grow, which pages hold attention, and which pages people leave quickly. Those patterns, not any single day's numbers, should guide what you publish next.

If you set up Analytics today, give it a month before you judge it. The first weeks of data are mostly noise while the tag settles and your own visits are filtered out. After a month, the patterns are real, and after three months they are reliable enough to guide decisions. Resist the urge to redesign your content plan on the basis of a single good or bad day.

Frequently asked questions

Is Google Analytics 4 free?

Yes. The standard version is free, and it is sufficient for most small and medium sites. There is a paid enterprise version for very large organisations.

How long before I see data?

Realtime data appears within seconds. Standard reports usually show data within a day, and some reports take up to 48 hours to fill in.

Do I need consent for Analytics?

In many regions, yes. Check the privacy rules where your visitors live, and use a consent banner if your jurisdiction requires one.
